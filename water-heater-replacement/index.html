How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater replacement touches more systems than most people expect. Gas work covers the supply line, sediment trap, shutoff, and a vent that must draft well enough to keep exhaust out of the house. On the electric side, the breaker and wire gauge must match the element wattage. The tank needs a relief valve of the correct rating piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models pile on more: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

A professional replacement covers the whole installation, not just the tank. We size the heater by first-hour rating or flow rate, verify the fuel, venting, and electrical capacity, and add the expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs. Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. If your current tank still has good years left and just needs a part, we will tell you directly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
