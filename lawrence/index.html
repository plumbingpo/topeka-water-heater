Keeping Your Lawrence Water Heater Running Longer

Many early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and is also showing rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Hot Water Headaches Lawrence Homeowners Face

Most water heater failures in Lawrence give warning signs well before the tank gives out. Look out for a popping or rumbling sound during heating, rust-colored hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. These are early signs of heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and when they're caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they end in a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon.
