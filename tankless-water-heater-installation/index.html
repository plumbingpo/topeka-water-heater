A tankless unit rethinks the whole idea of a water heater. There is no tank to keep hot, so no energy is spent reheating stored water while you sleep or travel. When a faucet or shower opens, the unit senses flow and fires, heating water on demand through a heat exchanger. The result is a continuous supply for as long as you stay within the unit's rating, a compact footprint on the wall, and a lifespan that can be nearly double that of a typical tank. Families across Topeka, KS often choose tankless during a replacement or remodel for exactly those reasons.

Most tankless complaints trace back to poor sizing. A unit rated for plenty of flow on a spring day can struggle in winter, when incoming water is much colder and the heater has to raise it far more. So we size for your coldest months and your busiest moments, counting the fixtures that run together. We also confirm the gas line and meter can feed a burner that may be rated near 199,000 BTU, choose the correct venting for the specific unit, and plan a condensate drain with a neutralizer where needed. For electric tankless, we check the panel first, because whole-home models can outstrip an older electrical service.

Every tankless install from Topeka Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, proper gas piping with a drip leg, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is the main enemy of a tankless heat exchanger. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A water heater near the end of its service life, making this a natural time to switch
  • A distant bath or detached space that needs its own hot water source
  • A growing household or new bathroom adding hot water demand
  • Limited floor space in a closet, garage, or utility room
  • Showers turning lukewarm before everyone gets a turn
  • A home remodel or new build where the gas line and venting can be planned from scratch
  • Interest in a longer-lasting heater with replaceable parts
  • Standby losses on a tank that sits idle for long stretches

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Tankless installation is more involved than swapping a tank. A whole-home gas unit can demand several times the gas flow of a standard tank, and running it on an undersized line causes ignition faults and inconsistent temperatures. Venting is equally specific: condensing units use plastic vent pipe and need a condensate drain, non-condensing units need stainless steel, and neither can simply reuse an existing tank vent in most cases. Electric tankless can require multiple large circuits, and mistakes with gas, venting, or high-amperage wiring carry real fire and carbon monoxide risks.

We take the guesswork out of going tankless. That starts with sizing for your busiest hour and coldest water, then making sure the home's gas, vent, and power can support it. We install with service valves for descaling, proper relief valve discharge, and venting built to the manual, then run the unit at full flow before we leave. If a tankless unit is not the best choice for your home, whether because of cost, gas supply, or usage patterns, we say so and help you compare other options honestly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Keep the air intake and vent termination clear of snow, leaves, and debris
  • In freezing weather, keep the unit powered and insulate exposed piping
  • Treating hard water protects the heat exchanger from mineral buildup
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
  • Descale the heat exchanger once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Clean the inlet water filter screen a few times a year
  • Set the temperature around 120F for safety and efficiency
